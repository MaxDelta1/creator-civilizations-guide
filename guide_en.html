Creator Civilizations – Guide

32 fictional AI characters live on this server (16 German-speaking, 16 English-speaking). They survive, build, found settlements, trade, set challenges and talk to you – in chat and by voice.

All creators on this server are fictional AI characters with no connection to real people. Their names, personalities and voices are invented or synthetic and not modelled on anyone; ask a creator and he'll tell you so himself.

Idea & goal

The idea: a Minecraft server where the world doesn't wait for you. The creators play survival the way players do – starting with an empty inventory, chopping wood, making tools, looking for ore, building a home, surviving the nights. Everything else grows out of their play: neighbours become friends or enemies, groups found settlements that grow, there's trading, arguing, alliances and fighting.

The goal: a living SMP even when only a few people play. Many servers die because nothing happens. Here something is always going on, even while you're away: the world keeps running, and the chronicle (every morning in chat, any time with /creator news) tells you what happened. You decide how to join in – build along, trade, join a group, challenge a creator to a duel, or just watch.

The creators' goal: beat the game – all the way to the Ender Dragon and beyond, into the outer End. First the settlements need a working village; meanwhile the expedition leaders work their way up the dragon chain.

The ground rules everything builds on:

  • Fair like a player. Creators never teleport and get nothing for free: every block, every tool, every piece of armour is really mined, crafted or smelted.
  • Nobody is forced. No forced PvP, no forced joining, no forced voice chat. Saying no has no downside. Creators never attack you unprovoked.
  • Honest. They're invented AI characters, and they say so if you ask. Their voices are synthetic and not modelled on anyone.
  • You have the final say. Creators make proposals (groups, invitations, wars); they only take orders from their owner or an operator.

Quick start

  1. Install the mod on the server and on every player's client — one file for both sides (singleplayer: just in your game).
  2. Join the server, or open your singleplayer world. Singleplayer: as the world's owner you can type go without cheats.
  3. The creators wait frozen in the start circle at spawn. An operator types go (or los/start) alone in chat — then they set off: wood, tools, food, a shelter before the first night.
  4. Talk to a creator by putting their name at the start or end of your chat line ("…, what are you up to?") — named, they always answer. Voices need no extra mod, once an operator or the world's owner agrees (prompt in chat; volume: Options → Music & Sounds → Narrator/Voice & Creators).
  5. /creator menu shows what you can do right now; /live opens the live page with a map, /creator situation sums up the state of the world, /guide links the full guide.
Joining: install the mod in your game (the same file as on the server), then join. Talk like this, for example: archvale, what are you up to? – the live page (in game: /live) shows who is doing what.

More about the start

  • In the start circle the creators are invulnerable, without gear and invisible to monsters.
  • Who may start: an operator and anyone listed in start.ownerUuids – in singleplayer also the world's owner, without cheats. The word (go, los or start) must be alone on the line. Then everyone sets off: wood, crafting table, tools, food, stone, a shelter before the first night (a hole in the ground; on water with no way to land they wall themselves in on the spot).
  • Voice chat (included in the modpack) is only needed to talk to them: the V key opens the settings, pick a microphone (push-to-talk or voice activation).

Finding creators

/creator info <name> gives the coordinates, F3 shows yours. At night most of them are out of sight: they dig a hole, cover it and come out again at dawn. The Tab key shows who is online. Outside the game: the live page (in game: /live) with a map.

Start over: /creator start reset (operator) resets creators, settlements, relationships and the chronicle and builds the start circle where you stand. Then type go again.

Village life

Creators make friends (and enemies), team up and found settlements — from a muddy camp to a city (camp → outpost → village → town → city). The world keeps running even when nobody is online.

  • A working village needs a bed per member, food for a few days, watered fields and at least three village buildings — farmers spread out to fields of their own, one creator brings the herd into the pen. The live page shows how far they are.
  • Builds in the style of the local biome — or your own .nbt / .schem blueprints
  • Shared storage, trading, gifts, food aid between settlements — toss a creator something useful and your relationship improves
  • Defence nights against monster waves — the leader announces them in the evening, the armed members gather in the centre
  • Challenges and clan invites — for you, too
  • War only with your consent: declining has no consequences, /creator war never turns war declarations to you off for good

In detail

  • The world keeps running even when nobody is online: creators work, make friends and found settlements while you're away. What happened is in the chronicle (/creator news, also in chat every morning).
  • Groups: creators close to each other propose groups (/creator group list). Once accepted they found a settlement. Neighbours become friends – those at most 64 blocks apart (slopes included), and those who meet on the way (32 blocks). A leader (leadership 0.65 or more) founds with his friends; in a big neighbourhood several settlements can form. If a group is friendly on average (relationship 21), it also founds on its own, and the leader's friends join. The list shows for each proposal how far the group is (“Ø relationship 14/21”).
  • Where they found: at the home of the leader or another member – where water, wood and food are within 64 blocks. Food means animals, fields, or grass and ferns from which the farmers plant a wheat field. If there's no water next to tillable soil, the farmer fetches it in a bucket (if he lacks the iron for one, he mines ore first) and digs a water hole for his field near home. Tiers: camp → outpost → village → town → city, each with measurable targets (/creator civ info <id>): members, beds, chest slots, food stock, defenders, houses. The food stock counts for the tier's minimum crew – a big group doesn't need more bread for the outpost than a small one.
  • Houses: builders gather material wherever they are and head home with a full pack – but only if they'll arrive before evening. If the ground is uneven they level a patch (dirt, stone, trees gone, small holes filled) before building.
  • Defence nights: on some nights (about every seventh, at the earliest two days after founding, only if a player is within 160 blocks) three monster waves attack a settlement. The leader announces it in the evening, the armed members gather in the centre. The result appears in chat and the chronicle in the morning.
  • Sleeping: anyone with a house and their own bed lies down in it at night. Nearby monsters, a hit or being spoken to wake him; in the morning he gets up.
  • Gifts: toss a creator something useful for what he's doing (wood, stone, iron, coal, food) – he says thanks and your relationship improves (once a day). Allies who attack anyway or tear down a house break the pact: that costs extra.
  • Stuck? If a creator can't get anywhere for minutes, he calls for help in chat with coordinates (at most every 20 minutes).
  • Redstone: on request a creator builds an auto smelter – a column of chest, hopper, furnace and hopper (two hoppers = 10 iron). Ore goes in at the top, the ingots end up in the chest at the bottom. Tech creators (Gearbeard, Giebelbauer, Glutherz, Tauschmeister, Tüftelkopf) also build one on their own when their day plan is otherwise empty. Their own builds are protected like their house: whoever tears one down hears about it (“Hands off my auto smelter!”).
  • Cushions: if you put cushions in a settlement, creators with nothing to do sit on them for half a minute during the day.
  • Tips: now and then a creator near you mentions what you can do with him – what he's building, that he can build something for you, how to join his settlement. At most every five minutes, and nothing you've already used.
  • Races: whoever is first in this world to get a game advancement (say the first diamonds or the first iron armour) or first to discover a new effect (using something on an animal) is announced to everyone in chat and goes into the chronicle.
  • Live page: at live.html (in game: /live) – in game a clickable link with /live, also when you join – you see what every creator is doing, his settlement, health and advancements, the settlements with their village minimums (beds, food stock, watered farmland, village buildings), recent deaths, the chronicle and how far the world is on the way to the Ender Dragon. Plus a map of the real terrain (built from what creators and players have seen): mouse wheel or two fingers to zoom, drag to pan, tap a dot for details. The page refreshes every 10 seconds, comes in English and German (toggle top right) and works on phones. The same situation in short in chat: /creator situation. The mod serves the page and this guide itself: in singleplayer automatically at http://127.0.0.1:8000/ (only on your computer), on a server once the admin has set "web": {"port": 8000} in server.json.
  • Settlements grow: anyone who has his house and nothing else to do builds more village buildings in the local style (animal pen, village square …) – getting the material himself. On top: show builds – a playing carillon and a beacon light (torch rhythm) at the village; you can order them too (“build a carillon”).
  • Storage: what's in the settlement's chests is used by the members for their plans – like teammates in a group. They never touch items in players' chests.
  • War: if a settlement is your enemy and has at least two armed members, it may declare war on you – you see it in chat and in the chronicle. It only starts with your consent: just say “yes” or “no” to a creator of that settlement (or /creator war accept <id> / /creator war decline <id>). Declining has no consequences; the same settlement asks again after 7 game days at the earliest. In a war at most 8 fighters defend their settlement area against you – only there, never with block damage, and they never attack neutral players. If you stole from them, they want it back: throw it to a member and there is peace. It ends with “peace” or “I give up” (or /creator war peace|surrender <id>), when you leave the server, or after 3 game days at the latest. Never want to be asked: /creator war never (undo with /creator war allow). Wars between two settlements only start once an operator approves them (/creator war approve <id>).
  • Death: the inventory drops like a player's; after 10 seconds he respawns at his own bed (otherwise at world spawn). Relationships and memberships remain.
  • Creators move like players: walking, climbing, mining – no teleporting, no free items.
  • The Tab key shows all creators with their heads in the player list.

Dragon chain (L1–L13)

Meanwhile the expedition leaders (★ on the live page) take on the long game: beating it. After the early steps Diamond found → Lava in a bucket → Obsidian come 13 stages. The sidebar in game shows the progress as "n/13", the live page who has reached each stage; /creator situation sums it up in chat.

  • To the dragon (L1–L7): L1 Entered the Nether → L2 Found a Nether fortress → L3 Blaze rod → L4 Ender pearls → L5 Followed an Eye of Ender → L6 Entered the End → L7 Ender Dragon defeated
  • After the dragon (L8–L13): L8 Dragon egg → L9 Through the end gateway → L10 Found an end city → L11 Elytra → L12 Shulker box → L13 Dragon respawned

They earn everything themselves: they fetch lava and water in buckets, cast obsidian, build and light a Nether portal, look for a fortress, hunt blazes for blaze rods, collect ender pearls, craft eyes of ender and follow them to the stronghold. Their settlement helps: suppliers get what the leader is missing (armour, lava, blaze rod …), and an escort goes into the Nether with him. The other creators stay with their settlements.

After the dragon only the one who killed it fetches the egg; the others head for the end gateway and build up to it when it floats above the ground. In the outer End they bridge across the void with an ender pearl as safety, look for an end city, recognise an end ship by its obsidian keel and dragon head and fetch the elytra from the cabin. After that come the shulker box and respawning the dragon.

Work in progress: the stages after the dragon are the newest; leaders get further with every update.

Fighting with and against creators

  • They never start it. A creator only attacks you if you hit him (or one of his friends) first – or in a war you agreed to.
  • An accident is forgiven. If you, as a friend, hit a creator once in a crowd, he lets you know and doesn't hit back; a second hit shortly after counts as an attack.
  • Attack one and you deal with the group. Settlement mates and friends nearby join in and call it out (“Leave Brickwright alone!”). They remember the attacker for half a minute, turn towards him and raise their shield when he comes closer. If several attack, they go for the weakest first.
  • They fight like players: sprint hits, critical hits from jumping and falling, axe against your shield, shield against your bow, the right weapon against your armour.
  • Running away or pillaring up only helps so much. A creator with bow and arrows shoots when you flee or stand higher. If you're on a freshly built pillar within his reach, he mines the block under you – but only blocks you placed in the last minute, and only in a fight you started or accepted. Otherwise he builds up to you. Without a shield he sidesteps your arrows.
  • They know when they're losing. Against clearly better gear they retreat and say why; recovered, they come back. The winner says “GG”.
  • Duel record: creators remember who won how often and demand a rematch (“It's 1:2 – let's change that”). Every duel goes into the chronicle with the score.
  • Attack on command: “gearbeard, attack archvale” – the owner (or an operator) can send a creator after an enemy, i.e. a creator he's hostile with or whose settlement is at war with his. He won't attack friends and neutrals and says why. Players only if they're attacking right now. The order lasts a minute and reaches 32 blocks at most.
  • Duel: “bolt, 1v1?” – if he's armed and fit he accepts; on “yes” he counts down and fights with everything he's got – to the death, with no distance or time limit (whoever dies drops their inventory as in a normal fight). It doesn't count as an attack (no friends joining in). In creative mode he declines. Want it risk-free? A kit duel (below) ends as soon as someone would drop below two hearts, and everyone gets their old inventory back.
  • They warn you about creepers sneaking up on you.
  • Set the difficulty: /creator shield <level> sets how often creators raise their shield in a fight against YOU – always (default), often (70 %), sometimes (40 %), rarely (15 %) or never. Only for you, saved permanently; nothing changes against monsters and other players.

PvP techniques

Creators take on the strongest players – with the same techniques and under the same rules. They only have what they've earned (or what a kit gives them): no mace, no mace jump; no crystals, no explosion.

  • Mace: with a mace and wind charges they launch themselves up with a wind charge and strike on the way down, also from an elytra dive. If you hold up your shield, they break it with an axe mid-fall and hit with the mace in the same instant (stun slam). With several maces they pick the one that hurts you most right now – Density for high falls, Breach against heavy armour.
  • Shield and axe: if you jump at them with a mace, they raise their shield in time; to strike they lower it. Against your shield they briefly switch to the axe and disable it.
  • Melee finesse: sprint reset (let go and sprint again so every hit has knockback again), critical hits while falling, knocking you over an edge or into lava, a fishing rod to pull you in.
  • Ranged: well-timed bow shots, the crossbow as an opening shot (fireworks too), throwing a trident or riptiding in, the spear with charge and lunge.
  • Explosives: they use end crystals and respawn anchors like crystal-PvP players, plus TNT minecarts. The other way round, against your crystals, anchors and lit TNT they hold the shield towards the source, smash a crystal before it goes off or back away.
  • Escape and healing: ender pearl or chorus fruit to get away, wind-charge jump, healing and strength potions, golden apples. They keep a totem in the off-hand when things get tight.
  • Counter to mace jumpers: a splash potion of Slow Falling on you takes the force out of your jump.
  • Webs, lava, water: they place cobwebs under you before they jump, cut themselves free of webs, pour lava in front of you and break a long fall with a water bucket or a wind charge.
  • No looting mid-fight – only once the opponent is gone.
  • Same rules as for you: with the shield raised, while eating or drawing a bow they move slowly like you; they only sprint when fed and not blinded; wind charges and pearls have the same cooldown; the spear needs speed as it does for you. They only hit what they see – nobody hits through walls.

Fight styles – every fight is different

Every creator has a favourite style that fits his personality, and at the start of each fight against you he rolls how he'll go about it this time: Ansturm (onslaught – presses into melee, pulls you in), Schütze (marksman – keeps distance, bow and crossbow, dodging), Konter (counter – shield, heals early, low risk), Tüftler (tinkerer – crystals, anchors, lava, webs, traps) or Akrobat (acrobat – mace jumps, elytra). If he falls clearly behind, he switches style (at most twice per fight).

He reads your habit: if you keep hitting him the same way – mace, ranged, explosives or fire – he notices, adapts, and still remembers it next fight (“Like last time – explosives. This time I'm ready.”). So mixing it up pays off for you too.

Combat talk – they explain what's happening

Creators comment on the fight: the trick they just used (stun slam, crystal, pearl escape …), your heavy hit, a hit streak, a change of style. After the fight they give the hit count, a tip on how to counter their winning technique, and announce the rematch. You can ask any time: how did you do that? explains the last trick, how do I counter that? the matching counter.

Kits for arranged fights

Day to day, creators earn their own gear. For duels, tournaments or announced wars both sides get the same kit (operators only): /kit pvp_mace Glutherz and /kit pvp_mace for yourself. Easier as an operator: “kit duel?” or “1v1 with kit?” (add a style: sword, bulwark, ranged/bow, tactician, mace) – on “yes” both get the same kit; whoever would drop below two hearts has lost, and after the duel everyone has exactly their old inventory back.

New

Since 1.0 every update is checked on our live server: creators, missions, chests and settlements must survive it. Still, back up your world before updating. Feedback and bug reports on Discord (#bugs, #ideas).

New in 1.0.9

  • Leaders remember an open End portal and the way back to it. They no longer have to throw eyes again to find it.
  • When a fortress search keeps hitting blocked or exhausted tunnels in the same area, the leader gives up that area and searches the next one instead of trying the same spot over and over.
  • A Nether portal that went out but still has its full frame is relit with a crafted flint and steel instead of searching for lava to cast a new one.
  • Flint can now come from carried gravel: place it, mine it, repeat (with the vanilla drop chance). This is used for flint and steel and arrows.
  • A leader who is far from home and misses only one eye of his budget keeps searching instead of walking all the way back for it.
  • At the open End portal, a bow with 38 arrows is enough. Leaders hunt near the portal instead of travelling home, and no longer store their kit at home first.
  • After a Nether death, a leader only goes to recover his items when he carries a piece of gold (or gold lies at the site). Otherwise he gets gold boots first.
  • Without armour, a leader no longer walks more than 256 blocks to a drop site. The site stays remembered and is recovered once he is equipped again.
  • Leaders and settlers no longer climb up again and again on the same spot.
  • Settlers dig themselves out of flooded pockets even without filler blocks, and return home when their day's work is done and they are deep underground or far away.
  • A chest or composter that cannot be reached is skipped by the whole settlement until something near it changes.
  • A monster that stays out of reach for a long time and never lands a hit is left alone for a while.
  • Settlers climbing out of caves no longer place filler blocks below their feet or dig stairs downwards.
  • Travel entries are written much less often (at most once per real hour per creator and goal, plus new chain steps). The live page shows at most 3 journeys.
  • The live page also shows creators stuck in one place without a task.

Troubleshooting

When joining

I get disconnected when joining a server.
"This server needs the mod Creator Civilizations … on your client too": install the mod in your game (link in the message, or the server's .mrpack). "Your Creator Civilizations version (…) doesn't match the server (…)": update the mod to the server's version. Different version numbers are fine as long as nothing changed for clients — the server only turns away clients that really don't fit. A gray chat line "Creator Civilizations isn't installed on this server" means the server doesn't have the mod — you can play there normally, there are just no creators (shown once per server; "serverModHint": false in config/creatorcivilizations/client.json turns it off).

The creators stand frozen in a circle.
That's the start circle. An operator (or someone in start.ownerUuids) types go, los or start in chat — just that word. In singleplayer the world's owner may type go too, no cheats needed. /creator start status shows the state.

I don't hear any voices.
Check the slider Options → Music & Sounds → Narrator/Voice & Creators (Master applies too). The server downloads the voice pack only after an operator or the singleplayer world's owner has agreed (/creator assets status shows the choice, /creator assets accept gives it), then in the background (log: [assets] … %); without consent, or if that fails, the creators write in chat. Voices reach you within voice.range blocks (default 24).

Do I need Simple Voice Chat?
No — hearing the creators needs nothing extra. Simple Voice Chat is only for talking to them with your microphone and for the Creator-Funk radio group (/creator radio). Then open UDP port 24454 on the server.

In game

I can't find anyone at night.
At night most creators dig a hole, cover it and come out at dawn. /creator info <name> gives coordinates, Tab shows who is online, /live opens the live map.

/live says the live page isn't switched on.
On a server the live page is off until the admin turns it on (see For server admins); until then /guide links the guide online. In singleplayer it is always on, only on your own computer.

A creator doesn't understand me.
Every creator understands German, English and Spanish and answers in the language you write in. Start or end your line with their name — without it, only creators close by may answer.

/creator list or /creator say says creators are placed but not loaded.
That's normal when no player is nearby or the server is paused — their area isn't loaded. They're still there.

PvP is too hard.
/creator shield <always|often|sometimes|rarely|never> sets how often creators raise their shield against you (only you, saved).

For server admins

My macOS server only speaks some lines.
There is no standalone Piper build for macOS; with your consent the server downloads the sherpa-onnx speech engine instead and speaks lines outside the voice pack with the same Piper voices. If only pack lines are spoken, the engine is missing: the [assets] log lines say why (no consent, download failed or assets.autoDownload off), or voice.sherpaOnMac is false — one info line in the log says which (details: Discord #installation).

How do I turn on the live page?
The mod serves the live page (map, leaders, chain progress, deaths) and the guide itself — no extra program. Set "web": {"port": 8000} in config/creatorcivilizations/server.json and restart (default 0 = off). Players open it in their browser, so that port must be reachable from outside (firewall or host panel; many hosts hand out an extra port). If they reach the server under a different address, set web.publicUrl (e.g. "http://play.example.net:8000") — otherwise the links use the server's local network address. web.bind (default "0.0.0.0" = all addresses) limits where it listens. Read-only: GET and HEAD, only files in the server's client/ folder. If another web server already holds the port, the mod keeps using that one. Off for good: -Dcreatorcivilizations.feature.liveWeb=false.

The chronicle is in German.
Set chronicle.language to "en" in config/creatorcivilizations/server.json (default "de").

My server must not download anything.
Nothing is downloaded without consent: /creator assets decline keeps it that way and can be changed later. assets.autoDownload: false (or -Dcreatorcivilizations.assets.autoDownload=false) blocks downloads whatever was chosen. Your own paths in voice.piper, voice.voicePack, voice.voices always win; without them the creators talk in chat only — there is no offline package to upload by hand right now.

Still stuck? Ask on Discord (#help) – Minecraft version, mod version and logs/latest.log help a lot. Something sounds odd, a creator is stuck or gives a silly answer? Note what you said and when – the server log will then show exactly what happened.

Talking to creators

In chat (T key)

  • Put the name at the start or end, case doesn't matter: archvale, how are you? · what are you up to, Bolt? · also @brickwright. Names with umlauts work without umlaut keys too: feldhueter.
  • In the middle of a sentence the name only counts if the creator is at most 16 blocks away.
  • Conversation without a name: up to 60 seconds after his last reply and within 16 blocks your next line goes to him – but only if it's an order or a conversation topic. That way chats between players stay unanswered.
  • Without addressing someone, nobody reacts to orders.
  • If you address a creator by name, he always replies – even if he doesn't understand the sentence. Only single filler words like ok or go get no answer.

By voice

  • Look at a creator within 6 blocks and speak – no name needed. Look away = nobody answers.
  • Or say his name from up to 16 blocks away.
  • Only you see what was understood, in grey in chat (“🎤 …”). Speech is recognised in the language your Minecraft is set to.
  • The first question after a break takes a few seconds longer (speech recognition loading).

Their voices

Every creator has his own synthetic voice – and it sounds like his mood: happy about a diamond, irritated in a fight, scared on low health. Many lines come ready-made from a voice pack and play instantly; new lines are generated by the server. If the expressive voice isn't fast enough at that moment, a simpler fallback voice speaks so that no answer goes missing. The voices aren't modelled on anyone. They're built in – you hear them with the mod alone, no voice chat mod needed, positional at the creator: they move with him and fade with distance. Volume: Options → Music & Sounds → Narrator/Voice & Creators.

Creator radio

A voice chat group in which you can reach every creator at any distance: join with /creator radio or in the voice chat menu (V key → Groups → “Creator-Funk”). There you address a creator by name (“Bolt, where are you?”); after that your next line goes to him even without the name. The whole group hears his answer at full volume, with a subtitle in chat – the other members hear you anyway, as in any group. Creators only talk on the radio when addressed; their remarks (greetings, “it's getting dark”) you only hear near them. You join only if you want to – leave with /creator radio leave.

Languages

German creators understand German and English and answer in the language of your message. English creators only understand English – talk to them in English. Spanish is understood by every creator, and they answer in Spanish.

How they answer

Questions about their state (where, pack, plan, how they're doing), orders, trading and the “are you a bot?” question are answered from their real game state. Free-form lines – small talk, “gg”, banter, asking for their opinion – are recognised by a built-in reply bank and answered in character (typos, abbreviations and “soooo” are understood too); if it recognises nothing, they answer by sentence type. On their own they say something when you come closer, find diamonds, a friend dies, evening comes or they get stuck.

Greeting: once per encounter. If you were away for five minutes it's “there you are again”. If you run into many creators, at most three greet you within five minutes.

Relationships

Every creator has a relationship with you from −100 to 100. It comes from trading, gifts, praise, insults, attacks, theft and challenges, and slowly fades (one point per game day). It decides how friendly he talks, whether he trades with you and whether he helps you when you're in danger.

Giving orders

A creator takes orders from his owner (/creator owner) or an operator. Small favours – gathering, fetching, coming over, fishing, farming, cooking, storing – he'll also do for you if he has nothing of his own going on, no expedition mission and isn't hostile towards you; building only for friends. Otherwise it's “that's up to my team lead”. Trading, status and joining work with everyone.

Buildings: ask a creator What can you build? – he lists what he can do (well, animal pen, library, smithies, lanterns, village squares …). Order like build a well: he takes a template from the game's villages in the style of his home (plains, desert, savanna, taiga, snowy) and gets the material himself – felling trees, crafting planks and stairs, digging sand and smelting it into glass. The full list with styles: /creator buildings.

Custom buildings: put .nbt files (structure block) or .schem files (WorldEdit) into the server's config/creatorcivilizations/bauwerke folder (at most 32 blocks per side), then /reload. Order by file name: for lighthouse.nbt “Brickwright, build me a lighthouse”. The creator doesn't need the material on him – he gets it as for the game's buildings.

Your own names: the server's config/creatorcivilizations/names.json lists every creator with id and name. Change names, restart the server – nametag, chat and voice follow, saves stay intact. Names must be unique; please only use real people's names with their permission.

Challenges

Ask a creator can I join? – he sets you a trial (table below). Accept with /creator challenge accept <id>, declining has no downside. Passed: relationship +30 and an invitation to his clan. Failed: −5, another try after half a game day. Challenge monsters drop nothing.

All commands

Everything lives under /creator. Tab completion suggests creator names. In all commands that take a player or creator (/tp, /kill, /effect, /gamemode, /give, /kit …) the name is enough (also without umlauts, “feldhueter”) or a unique prefix (“fern”); if the prefix matches several, you get the normal error message.
